# Current AI & Space-Technology Story Options

**Audience:** Carlos / Space Exploration News editorial review  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-04  
**Status:** Draft for editorial selection — not for publication

## Recommended lead: AI has started planning mobility on another world

**Verified fact.** NASA reported that Perseverance completed two Mars drives on December 8 and 10, 2025 using generative AI to create route waypoints, without human route planners providing those waypoints. [NASA/JPL, 2026-01-30](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/nasas-perseverance-rover-completes-first-ai-planned-drive-on-mars/).  
**Analysis / proposed angle.** The worthwhile story is not “AI drives a rover,” but which decisions are being delegated, why latency makes that useful, and what human review remains. It is an accessible entry point to perception, localization, planning, and control for a programmer audience.  
**Format fit.** 8–10 minute YouTube explainer + a small Python notebook illustrating grid/path planning (clearly a teaching simulation, not Perseverance flight code).  
**Editorial question for Carlos.** Is the on-camera angle “what generative AI can—and cannot—be trusted to do in remote robotics?”

## Option 2: A NASA–IBM geospatial foundation model has operated in orbit

**Verified fact.** NASA says its open-source Prithvi Geospatial foundation model was uploaded and demonstrated on two in-orbit platforms by a team including Adelaide University, ESA Φ-lab, Thales Alenia Space, and SmartSat; NASA describes it as the first geospatial foundation model deployed in orbit. [NASA Science, 2026-05-07; updated 2026-05-13](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/ai-foundation-model-in-orbit/).  
**Analysis / proposed angle.** This is a concrete “edge AI in space” story: moving inference closer to the sensor can reduce downlink pressure and shorten the time between an observation and a decision. Explain the distinction between a foundation model, fine-tuning, and flight validation.  
**Format fit.** News explainer + companion article diagramming the on-orbit inference pipeline.  
**Editorial question for Carlos.** Should the episode focus on Earth-observation use cases, or use this as the gateway to autonomous science missions farther from Earth?

## Option 3: Satellites navigating without GPS—and preparing to work as a swarm

**Verified fact.** NASA’s Starling mission demonstrated FALCON, which uses onboard star-tracker cameras and an onboard catalog of known satellites for GPS-independent self-orbit determination and catalog updates. NASA says a later 2026 experiment will have Starling’s four spacecraft share tracking data. [NASA Small Satellite Missions, 2026-08-17](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/smallsatellites/2026/08/17/nasas-starling-mission-opens-new-frontiers-in-space-navigation/).  
**Analysis / proposed angle.** The compelling story is the technical infrastructure of a lunar economy: navigation, distributed sensing, and collision awareness—not just rockets. It is also an approachable visual episode about coordinate frames, star trackers, sensor fusion, and networked autonomy.  
**Format fit.** 8-minute technology explainer + Python visualizer for relative navigation concepts.  
**Editorial question for Carlos.** Frame this primarily as a Moon/Mars-enabler, or as a space-traffic-management technology with near-term commercial relevance?

## Option 4: AI detects tsunamis using signals from navigation satellites

**Verified fact.** JPL says its experimental GUARDIAN system used AI-powered detection algorithms to identify ionospheric disturbances eight minutes after the July 29, 2025 Kamchatka earthquake, and flagged an incoming wave near Kauai 32 minutes before tide gauges detected landfall. [NASA/JPL, 2026-03-23](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/see-nasas-guardian-catch-a-tsunami/).  
**Analysis / proposed angle.** This is an excellent counterexample to “space AI is only about distant planets”: orbital/navigation infrastructure can become an early-warning sensor network for Earth. The episode should carefully distinguish an experimental additional warning tool from an operational public-alert authority.  
**Format fit.** News short or 6–8 minute explainer with an animated signal-path graphic.  
**Editorial question for Carlos.** Does the channel want a recurring “space tech improving life on Earth” lane?

## Option 5: NASA’s AI strategy: innovation paired with validation and accountability

**Verified fact.** NASA’s FY26–FY28 AI Strategy describes a three-year path emphasizing innovation, governance, public trust, prototyping, rigorous validation, transparency, explainability, and accountable oversight. [NASA, 2025-11](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/nasa-ai-strategy-official.pdf). NASA also publishes a Framework for the Ethical Use of AI. [NASA, accessed 2026-09-04](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-artificial-intelligence-ethics/).  
**Analysis / proposed angle.** Pair this policy story with the Perseverance and Prithvi examples: flight AI needs different standards from a consumer chatbot because operators cannot immediately intervene. The risk is that a policy-only episode may feel abstract; anchor it in a single real system.  
**Format fit.** Commentary/interview-style video or a written editorial; less suitable as the first standalone news episode.  
**Editorial question for Carlos.** Would a “how to evaluate AI claims in space” framework strengthen the channel’s authority?

## Option 6: Can AI help protect astronaut health on long missions?

**Verified fact.** NASA Ames’ AI for Life in Space group says it develops AI/ML frameworks to model, predict, and mitigate spaceflight risks; its listed focus areas include large language models/foundation models for real-time health decision-making and knowledge-graph work. [NASA Ames, accessed 2026-09-04](https://www.nasa.gov/ames/space-biosciences/research-branch/artificial-intelligence-for-life-in-space/).  
**Analysis / proposed angle.** The stronger version is a cautiously framed future-facing explainer: what data, validation, privacy, and fail-safe thresholds would be required before a clinical decision-support model could be trusted on a Mars mission? Avoid presenting research aims as an operational astronaut medical system.  
**Format fit.** Thoughtful explainer with a “what would have to be true?” structure.  
**Editorial question for Carlos.** Is the desired tone optimistic/technical, or more critical about safety and governance?

## Suggested sequencing

1. **Perseverance AI-planned drive** — clearest, most visual, and directly tied to exploration.
2. **Prithvi in orbit** — timely foundation-model/edge-compute follow-up.
3. **Starling GPS-independent navigation** — extends the autonomy series into distributed systems.
