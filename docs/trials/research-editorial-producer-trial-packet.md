## Assignment

Prepare a compact, evidence-led editorial package on this proposed topic:

> **When a Mars rover uses generative AI to plan a drive, what is the AI deciding—and what remains under human control?**

### Why this topic now

**Verified fact.** NASA reported on 2026-01-30 that Perseverance completed two Mars drives (December 8 and 10, 2025) using generative AI to create route waypoints, without human route planners supplying those waypoints. The timely hook is a concrete, recent example of generative AI being used for remote-mobility planning on another planet.

| [NASA/JPL: *NASA’s Perseverance Rover Completes First AI-Planned Drive on Mars*](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/nasas-perseverance-rover-completes-first-ai-planned-drive-on-mars/) | 2026-01-30 | 2026-09-04 | Supports the dates, generative-AI waypoint-planning description, and the boundary that human route planners did not provide those waypoints. Do not extend this claim into an assertion of unsupervised mission operation. |

**Proposed general-audience angle.** Rather than treating this as “AI drives a rover,” explain the handoff: maps and constraints become candidate route waypoints; the rover still has to navigate terrain safely; and mission teams retain responsibility for operations and risk decisions. Make the distinction between planning, navigation, autonomy, and human oversight understandable without assuming a technical background.

**Open question for Carlos.** Does this angle accurately reflect the editorial lane Carlos wants: a cautious explainer about what remote-robotics decisions can be delegated, rather than a broad claim that generative AI has made Mars exploration autonomous?
